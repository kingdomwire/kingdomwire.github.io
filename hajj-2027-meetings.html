The Year-Round Season

The Ministry of Hajj and Umrah has opened preparatory meetings with Hajj offices from more than 75 countries, nine months before the next pilgrims arrive. The season now runs all year, and the early start is the method, not the exception.

Hajj17 August 2026·4 min read·KingdomWire

The last pilgrims of the 2026 season are barely home, and the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah has already begun preparing the next one. Preparatory meetings with the Hajj affairs offices of more than 75 countries opened this week, reviewing operational plans and regulations for a season that arrives next spring. The gap between seasons, once a quiet interval, has become the most consequential phase of the calendar. Hajj administration in the Kingdom now runs all year, and the announcements of the past week show the sequence it follows.

The ministry describes the meetings as working sessions: operational plans reviewed, key instructions and regulations addressed, requirements discussed, and each delegation’s questions answered so that arrangements are completed according to approved timelines. The stated aim is coordination and readiness, smoother journeys and better services, under the Pilgrim Experience Program. It is the widest early consultation the ministry runs, and its opening this early sets the tone for every contract that follows.

Standards First, Then Sales

The meetings follow by days the Ministry of Tourism’s updated accommodation rules for the 2027 season, which sorted pilgrim housing in Makkah and Madinah into economy and first-class grades, inspected before licensing, before opening and in season. The order is the method. Standards are published first; the countries that contract against them are briefed second; the packages sold to pilgrims abroad are built third. A national Hajj office booking rooms for next spring now knows exactly what an economy or first-class facility must provide, and Saudi regulators know when they will inspect it.

Capacity on the ground is being read the same way. Prince Saud bin Mishaal, deputy governor of Makkah Region, reviewed readiness at Jeddah’s King Abdulaziz International Airport this week with Mazen Johar, chief executive of Jeddah Airports Co. The airport handled about 28.55 million passengers on some 169,900 flights between January and July. That number frames the planning problem: Hajj arrival waves no longer land at a seasonal facility that pauses for them, but inside a commercial hub running at record volumes all year, which is why airport readiness reviews now open the cycle rather than close it.

What the Early Start Buys

The return on starting in August is contracting certainty. Most of the failures that can mar a season, unlicensed housing, late visa batches, missed charter slots, are made months earlier in origin countries, in decisions the Kingdom does not directly control. Meeting 75 national offices now is how the ministry reaches into those decisions while they are still open. Last season’s numbers explain the confidence: 1.7 million pilgrims performed the Hajj, 1,546,655 of them from abroad, and the tourism ministry logged more than 19,000 accommodation inspections. The oversight apparatus exists; the meetings move it upstream.

The markers to watch through the autumn are the bilateral arrangements signed out of these sessions, the opening of package sales on the ministry’s platforms, and how the two accommodation grades price in Makkah’s booking market. Each season now begins earlier than the one before, and the 2027 cycle has opened earliest of all. The Kingdom is treating Hajj capacity less as an annual event to be staged than as a permanent system to be run, and the system’s first output, certainty, is already being distributed to 75 capitals.
