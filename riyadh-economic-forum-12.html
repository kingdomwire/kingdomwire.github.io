The Five Studies

The 12th Riyadh Economic Forum convenes Monday through Wednesday under King Salman’s patronage, with five commissioned studies: urban congestion, knowledge localization, smart agriculture, the legislative environment, and the economics of hosting Expo 2030 and the 2034 World Cup.

Economy11 October 2026·4 min read·KingdomWire

Read as a list, the agenda of the 12th Riyadh Economic Forum is a self-diagnosis. None of the five studies the forum presents this week is about raising capital or announcing projects. All five are about conversion: moving people through cities that have outgrown their roads, moving knowledge from imported expertise into Saudi cadres, moving water and technology into food security, moving legislation at the speed of the economy it regulates, and moving two global events, Expo 2030 and the 2034 World Cup, from calendar dates into permanent value chains. The buildout is funded. The questions the forum has chosen are throughput questions.

The confirmed facts come from the forum and the Saudi Press Agency. The 12th edition runs from Monday, October 12, through Wednesday in Riyadh, under the patronage of King Salman. The board of trustees chair, Abdulaziz Al-Anqari, said the patronage reflects the leadership’s support for initiatives that strengthen the national economy and the private sector’s role, and described the forum as a partnership among government, business and academia. Five strategic studies anchor the sessions: traffic congestion in major cities and its economic costs; the transfer and localization of knowledge among national cadres; smart agriculture and artificial intelligence in food and water security; the effect of legislation and public policy on sustainable development, including the efficiency of the regulatory and judicial environment; and maximizing the returns from hosting major global events.

The forum has been running since 2003, and its previous editions have produced 57 studies and 384 recommendations, a record that functions as a long correspondence with the state. Its format is unusual in the region: commissioned research presented to ministers and executives, with recommendations that feed the government’s own planning cycle rather than a communique written for the cameras. The timing of this edition sharpens that function. Its output lands in the window before the full 2027 budget, due around December, and beside a fiscal plan that already commits SR1.392 trillion in spending against a deficit the Finance Ministry wants narrower.

Each study maps to a file the government is already working. The congestion study arrives as Riyadh’s metro adds a second fleet order and the capital’s infrastructure permits run at record levels; its real subject is whether the city’s growth model can price and route its own success. The knowledge localization study is the labor file by another name: the Kingdom has spent a decade importing expertise and the last two years adjusting visa rules, most recently to let skilled expatriates bring families concurrently, and the open question is how much of that expertise transfers before it departs. The smart agriculture study sits on the water file, where an SR8.5 billion pipeline from Jubail to Buraydah is under construction precisely because food security and water scarcity are the same problem stated twice.

The legislation study is the quietest and the most consequential. The Council of Economic and Development Affairs reviewed a draft clinical studies law and a justice sector strategy this month; the forum’s question, how efficiently the regulatory and judicial environment converts policy into enforceable rules, is the question every foreign investor asks in private. A study presented under royal patronage, with ministries in the room, is a mechanism for asking it in public.

The events study carries the largest numbers. Expo 2030 and the 2034 World Cup are committed expenditures; the study asks what remains when the closing ceremonies end. The useful comparison is not with other hosts’ stadiums but with the Kingdom’s own record on converting one-time platforms, seasons, exhibitions, summits, into standing industries. The falconry exhibition that closed this weekend built an auction market in six years. The forum is asking whether the same conversion logic scales four orders of magnitude.

What to watch from Monday is uptake, not applause: which ministries adopt which recommendations, whether the congestion study puts a riyal figure on the capital’s lost hours, and whether the legislation study names the bottlenecks that private briefings already name. The forum’s value has always been that its papers outlive its panels. This year the papers arrive exactly when the budget drafters are reading.

Reporting basis: Saudi Press Agency statements and forum materials; Asharq Al-Awsat.
