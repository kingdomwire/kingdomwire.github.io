The Ninety-Sixth Year

The Kingdom marks its 96th National Day with fireworks in nine cities, a mandated visual identity and a slogan that points at the land itself. The centenary now shares a year with Vision 2030.

National Day23 September 2026·4 min read·KingdomWire

The way Saudi Arabia produced its 96th National Day says as much as the celebration itself. Fireworks in nine cities at the same hour. Air shows staged across three regions on a published schedule. A visual identity, launched by the General Entertainment Authority in July under the slogan Our Pride Lies in Our Nature, that government, private and nonprofit entities are required to apply according to approved guidelines. The anniversary of the Kingdom’s unification is now organized by the same machinery the state built to run its events economy, and the precision of the choreography is itself a measure of how far that machinery has come.

The day, a public holiday across the public, private and nonprofit sectors, followed a national geography. Riyadh’s landmarks and main roads were lit in green, and the capital’s municipality reported placing more than 12,000 flags in the city and over 53,000 across the region’s governorates. The Royal Saudi Air Force and the Saudi Falcons flew over Riyadh for a second day, after a three-day show in Jeddah that put a Saudia Boeing 787-9 and a flyadeal A320neo alongside the fighter displays. Naval and Border Guard displays ran on the Red Sea coast at Jeddah, with events in Yanbu, Abha and Al Birk, and fireworks were set for nine in the evening from Arar in the north to Al Baha in the southwest. Al Khobar’s air shows follow this weekend, with the first National Day participation by Aloula Aviation.

The slogan does something earlier themes did not: it locates national pride in geography rather than history. That choice tracks where the state has been directing attention and capital for a decade, AlUla and the Red Sea coast as tourism anchors, heritage registers, wildlife reserves and reintroduction programs, dark-sky designations on the coast. A national story long told through what lay beneath the land is being retold through the land itself, and the retelling has an economic program attached to it.

On the eve, King Salman chaired the Cabinet in Jeddah and gave thanks for “the great blessings bestowed upon this country, and for the honor of serving the Grand Mosque”, wishing the nation further achievements in its journey of growth and progress. The session’s agenda made the year’s point in miniature: alongside the anniversary, ministers noted the launch of Ceer’s first vehicles and a Riyadh biotechnology summit that produced more than 40 agreements. The 96th year closes with markers the 95th did not have. A new low-cost carrier is flying from Dammam. A Saudi carmaker is taking orders. Saudi films have passed SR100 million at the domestic box office for a second consecutive year, and Riyadh has entered a global top-50 city index for the first time. National Day increasingly functions as an annual reading of the delivery ledger.

The date itself commemorates the September 1932 royal decree by which King Abdulaziz united the Kingdom. The institutions that era produced, among them the ministries of foreign affairs, education and health, were the scaffolding of a state under construction. The institution staging this year’s anniversary, an entertainment authority with a brand manual and a nine-city fireworks schedule, is the product of a finished one turning to different work.

The calendar gives the next stretch its shape. The 100th National Day falls in 2030, the year Vision 2030 reaches its stated horizon, and the two clocks now run together. Each of the next four anniversaries will be read against the program’s remaining distance: the visitor numbers, the hotel rooms, the factories, the rankings. The centenary is no longer a distant ceremonial date. It is a deadline the state has set for itself twice.
