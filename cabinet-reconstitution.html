The Continuity Decree

King Salman reconstituted the Council of Ministers for a new four-year term with every minister retained, and moved exactly two chairmanships: the capital markets regulator and the local content authority. The names that did not change are the message; the two that did name the next phase.

Government14 August 2026·4 min read·KingdomWire

A royal decree issued Thursday reconstituted the Council of Ministers under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman as prime minister, with every current member retained. The step itself was required: the Council of Ministers Law sets a four-year term, and the clock had run out. The outcome was not required. A full-term reconstitution is the natural moment to change direction, reward performance or retire fatigue, and governments elsewhere treat it that way. The Kingdom used it to certify that the direction is settled. The only movement came in two companion orders, at the Capital Market Authority and the Local Content and Government Procurement Authority, and the pairing carries the signal: one body governs how Saudi projects are financed, the other how much of their value stays in the country.

Alongside the reconstitution, King Salman appointed Mazen Al-Sudairi chairman of the Capital Market Authority at the rank of minister, succeeding Mohammed El-Kuwaiz, who had led the regulator since 2017. A further order handed the chairmanship of the Local Content and Government Procurement Authority to Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman, the energy minister, relieving Bandar Al-Khorayef, who continues as minister of industry and mineral resources. Al-Sudairi arrives from inside the machinery: an adviser at the Cabinet's General Secretariat since 2024, before that head of research at Al Rajhi Capital, with earlier analyst posts at Alistithmar Capital and Samba Capital.

The retained cabinet is the one that has carried Vision 2030 into its delivery phase, and the calendar explains the choice. The four years that begin now end in 2030. The ministers kept in place hold the program portfolios, housing, tourism, industry, transport, whose targets sit inside this term, and swapping operators mid-build costs schedule, the scarcest resource in the Saudi project economy. Keeping every chair occupied reads as a decision about time.

Two Regulators, One Direction

What changes at the Capital Market Authority is profile. El-Kuwaiz's nine years were the era of opening: foreign investors admitted in stages, index inclusion secured, and a listings expansion that made Tadawul the region's dominant exchange. Al-Sudairi is a markets researcher elevated with ministerial rank, and the elevation of the post says as much as the name. The assignment ahead differs from the one just completed: less about admitting investors than about giving them more to buy, deepening the debt market, and making the exchange carry more of the financing load that the state and the banks have shouldered through the buildout so far.

The energy minister taking the local content chair concentrates procurement policy under the official whose sector wrote the Kingdom's localization playbook, through supplier development programs that predate the authority itself. The extension arrives at a specific moment. Local content in government procurement has passed 51 percent, from 28 percent in 2018, more than SR3 trillion of projects are expected to reach the market over three years, and 1,648 foreign contracting firms are registered to bid for them. The chair decides how hard the mandatory lists and price preferences press on that pipeline.

The appointments will be read through their first moves: whether the CMA's autumn agenda leans toward new listings or new instruments, and whether the procurement lists lengthen ahead of the stadium and Expo tenders. The larger fact requires no reading. The cabinet retained on Thursday is the cabinet that will stand at the podium in 2030, and it now owns the deadline it was assembled to meet.
