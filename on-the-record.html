Saudi government ministers, the royal court, and government entities in their own words.

MJ
Maram Al-Johani
Chief of Staff and Secretary General to the Board, PIF · 2025 annual report
17 Aug 2026 · Riyadh
"Throughout 2025, PIF continued to drive Saudi Arabia's economic development and diversification through long-term investments and the launch of strategic companies."
The Public Investment Fund's 2025 annual report puts revenue at $120 billion, up 9 percent, with net profit more than doubled to $17 billion and assets under management above $900 billion at year-end, from about $530 billion in 2021. The report records 80 percent of assets held inside the Kingdom, cumulative domestic deployment above $199 billion since 2021, and new offices in Paris, Beijing and Shanghai. Al-Johani says the fund's next strategic phase is an evolution toward six interconnected domestic ecosystems.

NA
Nasr Alansari
Spokesman, Ministry of Tourism · New Hajj accommodation rules
15 Aug 2026 · Riyadh
The updated regulations "strengthen the regulatory and operational framework and ensure facilities are fully prepared before receiving pilgrims."
The Ministry of Tourism approved updated rules for Hajj-season accommodation in Makkah and Madinah ahead of the 1448 season, sorting facilities into economy and first-class grades, each with defined standards for rooms, common areas, reception, cleanliness, maintenance and guest services. Facilities are inspected before licensing, before opening and through the season; the ministry logged more than 19,000 inspections of pilgrim accommodation last season and has told operators to complete maintenance and apply early.

MA
Mohammed Al-Ajlan
Chairman, Saudi Contractors Authority · On foreign entrants
11 Aug 2026 · Riyadh
"Saudi Arabia has become a destination for contractors and projects due to its growing international presence."
The Saudi Contractors Authority counts 1,648 foreign contracting firms in the Kingdom's market, on a register of 140,000 companies, and expects more than SR3 trillion of projects to reach the market over the next three years. In televised remarks, Al-Ajlan said foreign firms receive no special treatment, describing them as partners of the roughly 18,000 classified Saudi contractors in knowledge exchange rather than rivals, and named financing and payment cycles as the working problems the authority is taking to government entities and banks.
