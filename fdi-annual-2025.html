The Gross and the Net

Foreign investors put SR159.1 billion into the Kingdom in 2025, a third more than a year earlier, while Saudi direct investment abroad nearly doubled. The annual ledger now runs in both directions, and which line matters depends on the question being asked.

Investment15 October 2026·4 min read·KingdomWire

The General Authority for Statistics closed the books on 2025 foreign direct investment on Thursday, and the bulletin reads differently depending on which line the reader starts from. Gross inflows reached SR159.1 billion ($42.4 billion), up 33.5 percent from 2024 and the strongest annual figure since the FDI ambition was written into Vision 2030’s scorecard. Outflows nearly doubled, rising 98.5 percent to SR77.7 billion. Net flows, the figure the balance of payments records, settled at SR81.4 billion, up 1.7 percent from SR80 billion. One release, three readings, and each answers a different question.

The inflow mix answers the question of where foreign capital believes the Kingdom’s growth sits. Construction led with SR34.5 billion, or 21.7 percent of the total, with manufacturing just behind at SR33.4 billion, or 21 percent. Administrative and support services, the staffing and facilities layer that large projects rent rather than own, drew SR22.4 billion. Taken together, more than four riyals in ten arrived in sectors that pour concrete or run production lines. Foreign money is following the physical build, not waiting for it to finish.

The country table says something about the form that capital takes. Luxembourg recorded the highest net inflows at SR24.4 billion, ahead of the UAE and Egypt at SR9.4 billion each. Luxembourg functions less as an investor than as an address: pooled funds and holding structures domiciled there carry money raised across Europe, Asia and the Americas. Its place at the top of the table is a sign that institutional capital, managed and regulated, is arriving through vehicles built for scale rather than through single corporate balance sheets.

The outflow line is the newer story. Saudi direct investment abroad rose to SR77.7 billion, led by manufacturing at SR25.2 billion, nearly a third of the total, with construction at SR14.8 billion and wholesale and retail trade at about SR11 billion. The same sectors that dominate the inbound table dominate the outbound one, which suggests the pattern is structural: industrial and contracting firms that scaled at home are now buying and building positions outside it. An economy that only received capital would show a different ledger. Two-way flow is what a maturing investment account looks like, and it explains why the net figure moved little while both gross lines moved a lot.

Stock figures give the cumulative picture. Total FDI stock stood at SR1.06 trillion at the end of 2025, up 8.3 percent, with manufacturing holding 27 percent of it, ahead of wholesale and retail trade, finance and construction. The UAE holds the largest country position at SR170.7 billion, followed by Luxembourg at SR125.3 billion and the United States at SR73 billion. The stock table still reflects two decades of petrochemical and industrial joint ventures; the flow table shows what is being added on top of them.

The program arithmetic is public. Vision 2030 targets $100 billion in annual FDI by the end of the decade, and 2025’s inflows cover about 42 percent of that rate. The updated Investment Law, in force since February 2025, had less than a full year on these books, and the regional headquarters program keeps adding multinationals whose capital tends to follow their relocation. Hamza Dweik, head of trading for the Middle East and North Africa at Saxo Bank, told Arab News the direction of travel is positive and that Vision 2030 has built a sizeable pipeline of investment opportunities. The remaining distance is what the annual bulletins through 2030 are for.

What to watch is composition as much as volume. The third-quarter print will say whether construction holds its lead once early giga-project phases hand over to operators; the 2026 annual bulletin will be the first with the Investment Law in force from January to December; and the outflow line, if it keeps doubling, will start to matter for how the Kingdom’s external accounts are read. The headline number measures attraction. The quieter numbers now measure reach.

Reporting basis: GASTAT annual foreign direct investment statistics for 2025 as reported by Arab News (15 October 2026), with corroborating coverage by Saudi Gazette; Vision 2030 target figures as cited in that reporting; prior KingdomWire coverage of quarterly FDI data.
